# Industrial boiler acceptance and 90 mm wood-briquette trial protocol
**Research addition 22 Nov 2026.** Important finding from owner-supplied December 2025 note: large briquettes are not automatically suitable for automatic feeding or every combustion arrangement; manual feeding can be hazardous and lower thermal efficiency. See [research source review](../RESEARCH/2025-12-13-BIOMASS-BRIQUETTE-DRAWBACKS-CRITICAL-REVIEW.md).

## Four trial gates
**Gate 0 — safety and technical acceptance:** buyer utilities and EHS sign off that trial fuel size, feeding mechanism, boiler protection and permit conditions are compatible. **Do not instruct buyer to hand-feed a boiler or disable interlocks.**

**Gate 1 — accredited or mutually agreed lab test:** produce documented sample of the actual wood species. Measure wet-basis moisture, ash, GCV, apparent/bulk density, diameter/length, abrasion/fines, sulfur/chlorine if buyer requires, batch traceability, and compare with buyer-specific specifications.

**Gate 2 — controlled fuel trial:** qualified boiler operator agrees trial duration/load, baseline fuel performance and emissions; log tonnes fuel, tonnes steam, GCV, ash deposits, clinkering, feed blockages, smoke/exhaust, manual handling, uptime, incidents and operator acceptance. Avoid claims of a guaranteed % efficiency improvement from a website case study.

**Gate 3 — purchase evidence:** buyer's technical signoff, signed trial acceptance, purchase order/LOI, **actual MT/month and ₹/MT** (clearly delivered/ex works and GST), forecast schedule and credit terms.

## Commercial margin calculation buyers should compare
**Cost per useful heat** = landed fuel cost (₹/tonne) ÷ [tested GCV (kcal/kg)×1000×actual boiler efficiency], adjusted for ash cleaning, handling and rejects. Wood-briquette sale price of ₹8,000/MT is **owner-reported assumption**, not every buyer's payable rate. Do not claim all pharmaceutical facilities reject agro briquettes; acceptance is process and equipment-specific.
