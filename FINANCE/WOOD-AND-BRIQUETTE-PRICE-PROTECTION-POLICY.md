# Fifteen-year timber and briquette price-protection policy
**Owner's starting observations:** ₹8,000/finished MT briquettes; ₹2,000/wet MT incoming clean untreated logs. Neither is a binding 15-year contract. This is a procurement/sales negotiation framework, not executed legal wording.

## Commodity exposure
One finished tonne takes **1.57895 incoming wet tonnes** at 40% incoming moisture. Thus a ₹100 increase in wet wood price increases manufacturing cost by **₹157.89 per finished MT**, before any extra dryer-energy or inbound freight; at full 1,000 MT/month it reduces EBITDA by **₹1.579 lakh/month**. A ₹100 fall in briquette sale price reduces EBITDA by **₹1.00 lakh/month**. If moisture rises to 50%, wood input increases to ~1.895 wet tonnes per finished MT and more water must be evaporated.

## Quote / contract controls
1. Sales contracts: separately identify base sale price, GST, freight to buyer, GCV/ash/moisture basis, weighbridge reconciliations, rejected loads, credit and rate-revision interval.
2. Wood supply: specify legal species/source, fresh/wet weight, delivered freight, moisture measurement method, bark/stone/metal exclusions, minimum monthly tonnage, pricing validity and legal transit papers.
3. Index proposal: reviewed quarterly using agreed documented wood/fuel/power/transport input indices and purchaser's acceptable boiler cost per useful heat unit. **Subject to mutual written agreement**, do not assume automatic cost pass-through.
4. Concentration cap: diversify timber suppliers and buyers; single site or permit expiry could destroy sales.
5. Contract economics must be computed on **realised** price after quality deductions, transport, rejected tonnes, credit loss and delivered wood moisture.
